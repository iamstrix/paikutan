For the person on the mic

Read the bold script. Instructions in square brackets are performed, never spoken. A and B are alternative lines; record them separately. Aim for a friendly barangay competition: spirited, clear, and playful.

Three takes per line: natural, excited, then your best interpretation. The filenames below start at t01; use t02 and t03 for repeats. Check a variant off after its takes are captured.

Swipe → settle → select

Let each trumpo announce itself.

Use a tiny, quiet version of its skill sound while browsing, then a fuller signature when selected. The player’s character speaks the skill call in combat. A trumpo spirit voice is an optional additional layer, recorded separately from these effects.

Suggested playback: one menu voice at a time; cancel pending previews on fast swipes; fade old ambience over 80–120 ms; avoid repeating the same character line within 2 s. No automatic ambience loop is needed. These are implementation directions for the planned recordings.

Before rolling

A clean, repeatable recording session.

Recording setup, filenames, and delivery
  1. Record in the quietest room available. Put the phone or microphone about 15–20 cm away, slightly to the side of the mouth. Keep its position fixed.
  2. Record a normal line and an excited line, then listen back. If the loud read distorts, lower the input level or step back a little. Keep the character’s energy in the performance.
  3. Leave about 0.5 s of quiet before and after each take. In a long session, say the cue ID, pause, speak the line, pause, and repeat.
  4. Capture 10 s of room tone at the start and end. Record one actor at a time; group crowd recordings come later.
  5. Deliver dry WAV when possible, mono, 48 kHz / 24-bit. A phone’s original M4A is also fine; keep the original file and let the editor export WAV.
  • Voice direction: natural Filipino / Taglish. Speak comfortably; character identity comes from pace, rhythm, and attitude. Keep the vowels and last syllables audible.
  • Pronunciation pass: agree on Kid-lat, Tik-ba-lang, A-gi-mat, Di-wa-ta, and Ba-ku-na-wa before recording. Keep the cast consistent; use your group’s natural pronunciation.
  • Folders: sound/recordings/<entity>/. Spoken lines use vo_; creature/effort sounds vx_; selection effects amb_. Each filename includes entity, event, variant, and take.
  • Example: vo_lola_champion_a_t01.wav. With an actor suffix: vo_lola_champion_a_jose_t02.wav. One take per delivered file.
  • Editing later: trim pauses, keep a little breathing room, match perceived loudness, and add tiny fades. Export separate game clips after choosing the best reads. Add reverb, pitch layers, and ambience in the mix.
Casting and character direction

Bata: eager street player, quick ideas, bright rhythm. A classmate can suggest youthful energy in their normal voice.

Lola Basyang: warm storyteller with excellent timing. Calm confidence; she has played longer than everyone else.

Tambay: relaxed kanto regular. Smile in the voice, easy delivery, sudden excitement when a move lands.

Kapitana: clear, welcoming, decisive. She organizes the game and enjoys winning as much as anyone.

Aling Nena: quick, witty store owner. Uses change, stock, and suki metaphors; proud of her trumpo expertise.

Announcer: energetic barangay emcee with crisp timing. Crowd: 3–6 classmates, slightly staggered, with varied distances. Record individual voices as well as a group.

Roster notes and scope

Canonical roster: js/data.js. Bata, Lola Basyang, Tambay, Kapitana, Aling Nena are the five selectable voices; Kidlat, Tikbalang, Agimat, Diwata, Bakunawa are the five trumpos. Generic actor names are used for announcer and crowd so recorded clips work with custom player names.

The model file retains older art labels: “Lola Nena” supplies Lola Basyang’s rig; “Mang Totoy” supplies Tambay’s rig; “Tita Marites” supplies Aling Nena’s rig. They do not need duplicate playable voice packs. Kuya Jun and Kuya Tanod are reserve model ideas below, explicitly P3. Kapitana uses the uniform rig with her own appearance.

Dog and hen are active arena hazards. The chick has a hatch event. Roaming chickens, the parked askal, and carabao are scenery. The buildings, vehicles, cart, and videoke can use optional nearby-human vocals and Foley; they do not need character win/loss lines. An egg gets a crack or hatch sound rather than spoken dialogue.

Creative lines are proposals matched to the current mechanics. Optional spirit voices personify the trumpos for flavor. Skill calls remain in the player character’s voice even if no spirits are cast.
