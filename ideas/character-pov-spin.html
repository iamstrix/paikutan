Character POV Winding & Transparent Spin Overlay

Feature Adaptation from JMDGG's tromPOW Repository for Paikutan: Trumpo Showdown

Category: Immersion, Mobile UX, & Visual Fidelity (DOST Level Up 3.0)

1. Concept Origin & Problem Statement

In teammate JMDGG's original tromPOW prototype, players viewed their avatar from a dedicated first-person perspective while preparing the top. Currently in Paikutan, the phone controller displays an abstract 2D circular canvas during the Paikot! (winding) phase, separating the player's tactile touch from the 3D world.

This proposal adapts JMDGG's POV concept into a unified Diegetic First-Person Winding Rig with a semi-transparent HUD overlay. Instead of drawing on an isolated flat graphic, players look through the eyes of their chosen character (Bata, Lola Basyang, Tambay, or Kapitana) as their hands physically wind the cord (pisi) around their chosen trumpo.

2. Core Mechanic & Visual Experience

  1. First-Person Camera Setup (POV Rig):
    • Camera positioned at character chest height (y ≈ 1.35m), angled 30 degrees downward.
    • The chosen 3D trumpo rests in the character's left palm, with the right hand holding the red nylon cord (pisi).
    • Background shows the edge of the chalk circle, asphalt pavement, and the player's toes behind the throwing line.
  2. Transparent Touch Overlay (HUD):
    • A minimalist, semi-transparent circular guide ring floats directly over the 3D hands on the smartphone screen.
    • Opacity is tuned to 40%–60%, keeping the 3D wood grain, metallic crown, and hand movements completely visible beneath the touch path.
    • A luminous tracer bead tracks the player's thumb/finger as they draw circular revolutions.
  3. Synchronous Hand & String Animation:
    • Each completed circular stroke on the touchscreen wraps one layer of pisi tightly up the tapered body of the trumpo.
    • The character's wrist and fingers animate in sync with stroke velocity.
    • As rotational velocity reaches high RPM (80%+ spin meter), friction dust particles and elemental aura sparks emanate directly from the tip and crown.

3. Three-Phase Camera Choreography

Phase Camera Perspective Visual Action & Transition
1. Paikot! (Winding) First-Person Close-Up (POV) Camera focused tightly on the hands and trumpo. Transparent circular HUD active. String wraps tighter with each circle.
2. Bitaw! (Throw / Release) Dynamic Tilt & Push-Up As the 3-second timer expires, the camera smoothly pans up toward the chalk arena. The character's arm draws back and thrusts forward into the throw.
3. Laban! (Clash & Steering) Third-Person Chase / Overhead Camera seamlessly transitions from behind the player to the tactical duel camera (or player's assigned seat perspective) as the trumpo contacts the ground.

5. Technical Implementation Plan

  • Asset Sourcing from JMDGG's tromPOW:
    • Port the low-poly hand armature and string wrapping geometry from tromPOW/js/entities/player.js.
    • Render hands in the phone controller's local Three.js scene (budget: < 800 triangles per hand to maintain 60 FPS on mid-range mobile devices).
  • UI Layering (play.html):
    • Maintain the existing touch detection algorithm in js/controller.js (which calculates angular velocity and circle neatness).
    • Set the #circle-canvas overlay background to transparent and overlay it over canvas#view using CSS layering.
  • State Machine Integration:
    • During phase === 'wind', set local camera target to character POV rig.
    • When transition event ev('throw') fires, lerp camera position from POV to throwing line over 0.6 seconds using exponential easing.

6. DOST Level Up 3.0 Judging Alignment

  • Originality & Polish (20%): Moves mobile phone game controllers away from flat mobile button interfaces into true physical simulation.
  • Gameplay Feel (20%): Connects the player's physical finger strokes directly to the 3D spinning physics, making the Paikot phase feel tactile and rewarding.
  • Theme Relevance (20%): Perfectly captures the nostalgia of learning to wind a real trumpo in the streets of the Philippines.
