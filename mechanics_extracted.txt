I. Competition Mechanics 
A. Eligibility 
Student Category 
1. Participants must be enrolled as high school or college students as of 2026. 
Students who graduated in 2026 are eligible for this competition. 
2. Each participating school may send any number of teams, composed of a 
maximum of five (5) students accompanied by one (1) faculty coach or 
adviser. The coach will only provide mentorship and guidance throughout the 
competition. 
3. Only one (1) entry can be submitted per team for the competition. 
4. The team must be endorsed by the head of the school or university. 
Professional Category 
1. Participants are open to independent professionals or studio-
based developers. 
2. Interested professionals must register a team composed of a 
maximum of five (5) members only. 
3. Only one (1) entry per team can be submitted for the competition. 
 
B. Rules and Guidelines 
1. Theme 
Participants must create a game using any known or unique gameplay mechanic 
that can be associated with the following themes: 
 Smart City 
 Disaster Risk Reduction and Management  
 Climate Change 
 Education 
 Circular Economy 
 Philippine Games and Sports 
 Board Games / Brain Games 
 
2. Esports-Ready Gameplay 
 The game may be played in the following gameplay: 
o Player-versus-Player (PVP) matches (e.g. 1v1, team vs team, free for 
all)  
o Player-versus-Environment (PVE) with leaderboards (e.g. fastest 
completion time, highest score, survival mode or other similar 
mechanics)

 The game must feature skill-based or knowledge-based matches that can be 
completed within a reasonable time frame. It must have a way to determine the 
winner at the end of each match or round. 
 
3. Platform 
Participants must create and develop a game using any platform that permits the 
prototype to be played on a browser, personal computer, or mobile device without 
the purchase of subscription or license. 
 
4. Tools and Game Engine 
The game can be made from any programming language or can be made via game 
engines such as Flash, RPG Maker, Unity, Unreal Engine, Godot, etc. 
 
5. Publication 
The game must not have been commercially published on any major commercial 
gaming distribution platform or application store such as Steam, Epic Games, 
Google Play Store, Apple App Store, etc. However, games published or uploaded 
to online platforms as prototypes or betas/alphas such as in itch.io are eligible if it 
was not commercially released or actively used in previous competitions.  
 
6. Prohibitions 
The game must not contain the following:  
• Material that violates or infringes another's rights, including but not limited 
to privacy, publicity, copyright, trademark, patent, or intellectual property 
and property related rights. 
• Footages, images, or artworks not created by the team unless such 
footages, images, or artworks are included as part of the team's chosen 
game creation platform or the team has obtained all rights necessary to 
include such footages, images, or artworks as part of their entry to the 
competition. 
• Material that is inappropriate, indecent, obscene, hateful, torturous, 
defamatory, slanderous, or libelous. 
• Material that promotes bigotry, racism, hatred, or harm against any group 
or individual or promotes discrimination based on race, gender, religion, 
nationality, disability, sexual orientation, or age. 
• Material that is unlawful, in violation of, or contrary to the laws or regulations 
in the Philippine jurisdiction. 
• AI Generated content that violates any of the above. 
 
7. Code Originality and Integrity

 All game logic, mechanics, and source code must be created by the 
registered team members. 
 The game’s source code must be original and must not contain: 
 Code from previously published or submitted games in any other 
competition. 
 Code copied or adapted from other developers without permission. 
 Code directly taken from open-source projects, tutorials, or 
templates that implement the core mechanics of the game. 
 Personal code libraries may be reused only if: 
 They were created entirely by the member/s of the team. 
 They have never been part of a previously published or submitted 
game. 
 Any reused personal code must be declared during submission. 
 Violations of these rules will result in disqualification or forfeiture of 
the prize if found guilty by the organizers and judges even after the 
competition. 
 
8. AI Usage Disclosure 
• Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI), including but not limited to Large Language 
Models (LLMs), generative AI systems, coding assistants, asset generators, 
and similar technologies, is permitted in the development of the game.  
• AI may be used for ideation, game design, programming, debugging, 
writing, asset creation, prototyping, balancing, testing, documentation, and 
other development-related activities. 
• Any AI-assisted or AI-generated content used in the game must be 
disclosed during the competition. 
• AI-generated content must not infringe upon copyrights, trademarks, 
patents, trade secrets, privacy rights, or other intellectual property rights. 
• The organizers may request supporting documentation, source files, 
prompts, development logs, or version-control records to verify compliance. 
• Failure to disclose AI usage, misrepresentation of authorship, or use of 
infringing content may result in disqualification or forfeiture of prizes

proper rights and permissions. 
 The entry does not violate any of the restrictions stated in Sections 6, 7, and 
8.

10. Registration 
Interested teams must officially register in the Registration Link provided before 
submitting the requirements. 
 
11. Submission and Requirements 
The teams must upload their entries through the provided link on or before the 
published deadline of submission. These shall include the following:  
a) Form 01: Game Development Team Roles – list of team members and 
their role on the project.  
b) Form 02: Team Waiver and Declaration of Originality 
c) Form 03: Asset and AI Usage Disclosure (Not applicable for those with 
fully original entries) 
d) Template 01: Game Title and Synopsis – a short write-up limited to 500 
words describing the game including its mechanics/gameplay.  
e) Game Trailer – a 1-2 minute .mp4 video teaser of the game that can be 
played in loop during events related to the competition 
f) Prototype/Demo – a 3–5-minute .mp4 video recording of the gameplay. 
Video may include an explanation on how the game works (can be voiced 
or have closed captions/subtitles) 
All entries and requirements shall be checked by the organizing committee. 
All submissions will be pre-screened before qualifying for the competition. 
12. Pre-Screening/Shortlisting 
 Each DOST Regional Office shall have the authority to shortlist and endorse 
entries from their respective regions for both categories. A maximum of three 
(3) entries per category may be endorsed. However, only the top-ranked entry 
from each region will be automatically included as a finalist in the national 
competition. 
 The remaining two (2) endorsed entries will be placed on a waitlist, to be 
considered only if other regions are unable to submit their finalists. The 
competition judges shall determine whether additional finalists from any region 
may be included.

13. Finalist 
The chosen finalists will present their developed game for final judging during the 
Game Demonstration and Judging Day to be held on 26-27 November 2026 
during the NSTW celebration at General Santos City. 
 
14. Travel and Accommodation 
 
The DOST as the organizing institution will cover the transportation of Two (2) 
representatives per team (1 Student and 1 Coach for Student Category, and 2 
representatives for Professional Category). They will also be provided with a per 
diem allowance to cover their accommodation and incidental expenses.  
 
15. Winner 
The winning team will receive cash prizes and will have the chance to further 
develop their games with the assistance of DOST Regional Offices and partner 
institutions.
